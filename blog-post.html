What makes a TV commercial truly memorable? Lessons from 35 years of production

📅 August 2026 👤 ADJP Creative ⏱ 6 min read
🎬

After 35+ years of producing brand commercials in Sri Lanka — for clients ranging from LB Finance to Elephant House to Sri Lanka's most celebrated jewellery houses — we've sat in enough editing suites, client briefings, and final screenings to understand what separates the work that lasts from the work that fades.

The answer is almost always the same: story.

The myth of the product-first commercial

Many brands arrive with the instinct that a commercial should show their product as much as possible. The logic makes sense: you're paying to advertise your product, so fill the screen with it. But this fundamentally misunderstands how people process advertising.

Human beings remember stories. They remember characters, emotions, moments of surprise or recognition. They do not remember a list of product features narrated over b-roll footage of the factory floor.

"The best commercial we ever made for a jewellery client had almost no jewellery in it. It was a story about a father watching his daughter get ready on her wedding morning. The product appeared in the last four seconds. It ran for three years."

The 5-second test

One internal tool we use at ADJP Creative is what we call the 5-second test. It's simple: if you can't describe what your commercial is about — not what it shows, but what it's about — in five seconds, it doesn't have a story yet. It has a sequence.

There's a profound difference between the two. A sequence is ordered. A story is purposeful. A sequence has shots. A story has meaning.

The tension principle

Great advertising, like great cinema, is built on tension. Not necessarily conflict in the dramatic sense — but tension between where a character is and where they want to be, between a problem and its resolution, between what we expect and what we see.

Tension is what keeps a viewer watching. It creates the micro-commitment — the decision, made hundreds of times in thirty seconds, to keep watching rather than look away.

What this means in practice

Before we write a single frame of a storyboard, we ask our clients: what is the tension in your brand's story? Not the features. Not the USPs. The tension. The thing that your brand exists to resolve.

  • A finance brand's tension: the gap between where you are financially and where you want your family to be.
  • A jewellery brand's tension: the weight of a moment — a milestone, a declaration, a memory being made.
  • A food brand's tension: the distance between an ordinary Tuesday and something that feels like a celebration.

When you find that tension, you find your story. And when you find your story, you find your commercial.

The craft question

Story is necessary but not sufficient. We have seen brilliant ideas destroyed by poor execution — bad sound, flat lighting, performances that don't land. And we've seen modest ideas elevated by exceptional craft.

At ADJP Creative, our position is that craft is non-negotiable. Every production decision — lens choice, colour grade, music tempo, the pause before the product reveal — is a storytelling decision. There are no neutral choices. Everything communicates.

This is what 35 years in production teaches you, if you're paying attention. Not a formula. Not a template. An attentiveness to the gap between what you intend and what you've actually made — and the discipline to close that gap before the final cut.

That attentiveness is what makes a commercial memorable. And it starts, always, with the story.
